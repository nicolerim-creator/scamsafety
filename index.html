Stay Safe from Scams

Simple lessons that help you spot the tricks scammers use — so you can protect yourself and your money.

You are not alone. Millions of people are targeted every year. Knowing the red flags is the best protection.

What you’ll learn

  • Phone calls & robocalls — don’t answer unknown numbers
  • Fake text messages (packages, banks, tolls)
  • Fake emails that look real
  • Toll and traffic fee scams
  • Tech-support pop-ups
  • Family emergency / “grandparent” scams
  • Romance and online friend scams
  • Fake prizes and lottery wins

Quick Safety Tips

Keep these in mind whenever something feels urgent or “too good to be true.”

Slow down. Scammers need you to act fast. Real companies and agencies give you time. Urgency is a red flag.

Always remember

  • Hang up on unexpected calls that demand money or personal information.
  • Call back only on a number you already trust (from a bill, statement, or the company’s official website). Never call a number that came in a suspicious call, text, email, or voicemail — those numbers belong to scammers.
  • Use a family code word. If a relative “in trouble” cannot say it, it is a scam.
  • Never click links in unexpected emails or texts. Type the address yourself.
  • Do not give remote access to your computer to anyone who contacts you first.
  • Talk to a trusted person before sending money to anyone new.
  • Government agencies will not call to demand immediate payment or threaten arrest over the phone.

Never pay this way if someone contacts you first

  • Gift cards (iTunes, Google Play, Amazon, etc.)
  • Cryptocurrency (Bitcoin, etc.)
  • Wire transfers (Western Union, MoneyGram)
  • Payment apps to people you do not know in person
